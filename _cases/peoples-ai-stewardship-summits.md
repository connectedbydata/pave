# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "A series of 5 one-day public dialogue\
  \ events bringing together members of the public with experts, innovators, entrepreneurs\
  \ and other stakeholders to explore hopes, fears and expectations of AI to enable\
  \ positive change and improve people's lives. The events were led by the Royal Academy\
  \ of Engineering held in partnership with, and co-hosted by, the Academy's Regional\
  \ Enterprise Hubs (in Belfast, Glasgow, Liverpool, Swansea and London) to create\
  \ a unique space for stakeholders to listen to local members of the communities\
  \ in which they are based.\n\nThe rapid proliferation of Generative AI technologies\
  \ over the past few years, and the breadth of their penetration into areas of public\
  \ and private life and society, has pushed the questions of safe and responsible\
  \ development of AI technologies to the fore. But how do members of the public feel\
  \ about the implications of the huge changes that AI may bring into work, health,\
  \ finance, transportation and many other key areas of modern day life? What are\
  \ their hopes and fears for these new tools? And how do they perceive responsibility,\
  \ safety, and trustworthiness?\n\nThis series of summits brought together diverse\
  \ voices and expertise to consider a wide range of views and to attempt to ensure\
  \ that the voices of those who will be affected by technological change are listened\
  \ to and heard by those developing, implementing and overseeing such technological\
  \ innovations.\n\nWhat did we hear?\nThe insights from the summits have informed\
  \ the Academy's policy advice, enabling consideration of public perspectives alongside\
  \ those of experts. \n\nThe key five insights are:\n\n1. Deliberating future of\
  \ AI: Both stakeholders and members of the public want to have more opportunities\
  \ for dialogue to hear diverse perspectives and have informed discussions on AI.\n\
  \n2. Environmental impacts: People want more considerations for the longer-term\
  \ impacts of AI such as water scarcity and existing infrastructures.\n\n3. Human\
  \ oversight and connection: One of the biggest fears was AI hindering or even replacing\
  \ human connection, especially for vulnerable groups. The need for more human oversight\
  \ throughout AI development was emphasised strongly.\n\n4. Regulation: Robust regulations\
  \ on local, national, and international levels for safe and responsible AI systems.\
  \ \n   \n5. AI must benefit everyone: AI should be used to actively reduce inequalities\
  \ and not do more harm those who are already marginalised."

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "These summits brought 148 members\
  \ of the public (recruited from a diverse range of socio-economic and cultural backgrounds)\
  \ together with 90 AI sector stakeholders (innovators, entrepreneurs, researchers,\
  \ policymakers and civil society practitioners) across 5 locations in Northern Ireland,\
  \ Scotland, England and Wales. It focuses on exploring people's hopes, fears and\
  \ expectations for safe and responsible AI -- where and how it could and should\
  \ bring benefit for everyone, not just a few.\n\nStarting in March 2024 and concluding\
  \ in July 2025, the series witnessed the growing public awareness of AI, as LLMs\
  \ took off, from passing awareness to a near universal experience of some level\
  \ of use. It provided an open and dynamic space for experts and non-experts to discuss\
  \ and explore what AI could bring to their communities and localities, both benefits\
  \ and drawbacks. People were able to debate what kinds of benefits should be retained\
  \ locally from AI innovation as well as at a more national and societal levels.\n\
  \nAfter each summit a visual and narrative summary was published which captured\
  \ the flow of the day's discussions in an accessible format -- presenting ideas\
  \ and issues in a way that almost everyone can understand and appreciate. 25 vox\
  \ pop videos were also published capturing the thoughts, perceptions and reflections\
  \ of many of the participants in each location. Together they represent an extraordinary\
  \ resource of what different people in different parts of the UK thought and felt\
  \ about AI during that specific time window.\n\nWhat made these summits unique?\n\
  \n\u2022 Place-based approach: Local AI experts, policymakers and innovators were\
  \ able to hear from members of the public directly in which they are based. Discussions\
  \ surfaced local contexts, needs, and strengths in technological innovation, providing\
  \ clarity on how they could be leveraged to promote impact-driven entrepreneurship\
  \ in the long term.\n\n\u2022 Inclusive: Diversity of views, quality of conversations,\
  \ and interaction between public participants and stakeholders provided valuable\
  \ insights on topics in relation to AI including health, work and skills, education\
  \ and transport.\n\n\u2022 Deliberative: Going beyond simple opinion sampling, the\
  \ summits created a deliberative space for all participants that enabled mutual\
  \ listening and the sharing of both expertise and lived experience.\n\n\u2022 Positive\
  \ futures: the summits not only enabled people to express their fears and concerns\
  \ but challenged them to deliberate together to articulate positive visions for\
  \ AI that could be societally beneficial, safe and responsible."
