# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "The Exploring Children\u2019s Rights\
  \ and A.I project (2022-2025), delivered in partnership with the Scottish A.I Alliance\
  \ and The Alan Turing Institute, was an internationally trail blazing investigation\
  \ into children\u2019s views of A.I and its impact on their lives and rights.\n\n\
  Over the course of the project, our team worked with 140 children aged 8 to 12 years\
  \ from 6 schools across Scotland. The children were optimistic about A.I possibilities,\
  \ but they had real concerns about the impact that A.I could have on their lives,\
  \ and they shared ideas for what needs to change now to prevent potential future\
  \ harm.\n\nThe project\u2019s outputs included films, reports, resources, and children\u2019\
  s Calls to Action."

# Headline findings
headline-findings: "The children developed 12 calls to action from the key issues\
  \ that had been identified by participating children in relation to A.I. and children\u2019\
  s human rights. These calls to action - written by the children in their own words\
  \ - reflect the changes that participating children consider necessary for A.I.\
  \ to play a role in supporting children\u2019s human rights in Scotland, with additional\
  \ protections to prevent harms resulting from the development and use of these technologies.\
  \ \n\nThe 12 calls to action are below. Also in a video https://youtu.be/Mwgpf21B1GE?si=8_M31Q_WT1v2Ao4h\n\
  \nFairness and Bias\n1. Children have the right to be included, to have a say, and\
  \ to be listened to. Adults need to ask children for their views when they are making\
  \ decisions about designing or using A.I. If it is only adults making A.I. systems,\
  \ the A.I. systems won\u2019t understand children.\n2. Lots of different people,\
  \ including children, should be involved in A.I. development. To avoid bias, they\
  \ need to take everybody\u2019s lives into account.\n3. Adults must ensure that\
  \ the use of A.I. does not have a negative impact on any children\u2019s rights,\
  \ for example the right to appropriate and accurate information, or the right to\
  \ protection from discrimination.\nSafety and Security \n1. There should be rules\
  \ about how much and what data companies are allowed to gather about children. Companies\
  \ should not collect or share data from children unless it is absolutely necessary.\n\
  2. Companies should not use children\u2019s data to train A.I. systems without children\
  \ being asked.\n3. Children feel A.I. can\u2019t always be trusted and isn\u2019\
  t always safe \u2013more child-friendly information about A.I. is needed, to help\
  \ children make informed choices.\nA.I. in Education\n1. A.I. might not understand\
  \ neurodivergent children and how they learn in different ways. Decision-makers\
  \ must take this into account when deciding what A.I. systems can be used in schools.\
  \ A.I. systems need to include and support all children and their human rights.\n\
  2. Teachers can use A.I. systems to help make learning fun \u2013 children learn\
  \ better when it\u2019s fun. Teachers should be supported to use A.I. appropriately\
  \ in class.\n3. A.I. should support, not replace, teachers. Teachers understand\
  \ children\u2019s feelings and children think this is really important.\nLearning\
  \ about A.I.\n1. A.I. should be in the curriculum. A.I. will be a part of all children\u2019\
  s lives, so they need to learn and understand what it means before they grow up.\n\
  2. More children should know about A.I. so they can understand what\u2019s happening\
  \ when they use it. This will help to make sure children\u2019s rights are respected.\
  \ The more children learn about A.I., the more they will know how to keep themselves\
  \ safe.\n\n1. Teachers should learn about A.I. and children\u2019s rights to support\
  \ children\u2019s learning and help to keep them safe.\n\n\n"

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "We worked with a range of schools across Scotland to capture urban,\
  \ rural and island communities.\n\nWe identified schools and children within those\
  \ communities who are both representative of the community and come from marginalised\
  \ groups including  those from minority ethnic communities, refugees and asylum\
  \ seekers and children with additional support needs.\n\nWe worked with the Alan\
  \ Turing Institute and skilled creative artists to help translate and articulate\
  \ complex concepts to children through playful and creative methods.\n\nWe used\
  \ a child rights approach ensuring children were able to participate  in a variety\
  \ of ways to suit their own skills and interests and in ways which ensured they\
  \ felt safe and supported.\n\nThis project demonstrated a children\u2019s rights\
  \ approach through: \n\nBuilding the capacity and agency of children as rights-holders\
  \ to claim their rights - we ensured children had a thorough knowledge of A.I. and\
  \ children\u2019s rights, and experienced being respected, listened to, and taken\
  \ seriously. Project planning and delivery was inclusive and child friendly with\
  \ fun, creative and easy to follow activities so children could explore the topic\
  \ and share their thoughts confidently. \n\nSupporting children to defend their\
  \ rights and those of others- we supported children to gather the views of other\
  \ children through class missions and surveys and to articulate their collated calls\
  \ for change with decision makers.\n\nBuilding the capacity of duty-bearers to fulfil\
  \ their obligations to children - we facilitated the direct engagement of children\
  \ with decision makers at the A.I. Summits and sharing events; the dissemination\
  \ of calls to action and resources ensured duty bearers had a clear understanding\
  \ of children\u2019s views on A.I. in relation to their rights. \n\n\nRecognising\
  \ relationships based in human rights values are necessary for realising children\u2019\
  s rights - we demonstrated the values of kindness, trust and respect for human dignity\
  \ and encouraged participating children and adults to do the same. \n\nEstablishing\
  \ an environment that supports children to participate and advocate effectively\
  \ for themselves - we demonstrated in practice how children can meaningfully participate,\
  \ disrupting adult-child hierarchies, and created an environment where children\
  \ felt able to share their thoughts and ideas openly. We collaborated with artists\
  \ throughout the process which provided children with new and hands-on experiences\
  \ and empowered them to express their thoughts and ideas. \n\nEnabling adults to\
  \ benefit from the rich learning intrinsic to a children\u2019s rights approach\
  \ - adults around the project - including parents and carers, teachers, project\
  \ and A.I. partners, practitioners, policy leads, academics and decision makers\
  \ - had the opportunity not only to see the calls to action and resulting resources\
  \ but to experience the power of rights-based practice "

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "This project deliberately centred\
  \ children and children's rights and applied that lens to the exploration of AI,\
  \ as opposed using the lens of AI. Using that lens ensures that children have a\
  \ say and have a right to be taken seriously in AI governance. It was recognised\
  \ internationally as being a ground-breaking initiative. \n\nThe work that the children\
  \ have done over three years provides a wealth of evidence on what children think\
  \ and feel about AI and its impacts. \n\nThe challenge of how to operationalise\
  \ the project\u2019s learnings from its engagement with the children remain and\
  \ this is an opportunity to help drive implementation. \n\nEnsuring that children\u2019\
  s voices are meaningfully represented in the A.I. discourse mirrors a wider structural\
  \ challenge in AI governance: moving beyond stated commitments to participation\
  \ toward sustained, influential engagement with affected communities. \n\nThroughout\
  \ the programme, it became clear that while the importance of hearing from children\
  \ was widely acknowledged, sustained and embedded mechanisms eluded us. For children\
  \ in particular, power imbalances, adult-centric decision making, and a reluctance\
  \ to commit to formal pathways into policy and design processes meant that their\
  \ insights and influence struggle to impact outcomes in a durable way.  \n\nChildren\u2019\
  s Parliament uses specific rights-based practice to create equitable spaces between\
  \ children and adults where children feel able to express themselves fully and creatively.\
  \ This approach allowed both for the complex nature of AI technologies to be explored\
  \ accessibly with primary aged children, and for those children to speak openly\
  \ about the ways in which they view these technologies as effecting the lives of\
  \ children - both positively and negatively."
