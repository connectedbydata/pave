# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "The Global AI Dialogues are a dialogue\
  \ series that invites citizens around the world to engage, discuss, and contribute\
  \ their perspectives on the topic of Artificial Intelligence (AI), specifically\
  \ generative AI and facial processing technologies. So far, the dialogues have taken\
  \ place in Bolivia, Mexico, Nigeria, India, Germany, and Japan. During the one-day\
  \ dialogue workshop, participants explore the deployment of technology in specific\
  \ contexts, reflect on its future implications for societies, determine whether\
  \ they consider its future deployment desirable, and identify relevant measures\
  \ to support its desirable deployment \u2013 or alternative measures in the event\
  \ that its deployment is not deemed desirable. In addition to providing spaces for\
  \ discourse, the Global AI Dialogues is an international research project comparing\
  \ participants\u2019 perspectives cross-nationally."
# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: One limitation of conducting dialogues
  is the necessary budget for participant compensation, food and drinks, and venue
  costs. This restricts either the number of participants per location or the number
  of locations where the dialogues can be held. For the Global AI Dialogues, for example,
  our financial resources limited the dialogues to six countries. For our multi-location
  dialogues, all implementing research teams applied the same sampling criteria and
  attempted to achieve demographic heterogeneity through targeted sampling. Given
  different age structures, university quotas, and factors such as familiarity and
  openness to participating in dialogues, the samples in the six countries are inevitably
  different from each other. Our priority was to apply the same sampling criteria
  and achieve a sample that resembled the location of the dialogue. We acknowledge
  that our sample ended up being rather educated.

# Headline findings
headline-findings: "The results from the dialogues were jointly analyzed by an international\
  \ research team, composed of the researchers who organized and facilitated the local\
  \ dialogues. The reported findings reflect the researchers\u2019 synthesis.\n\n\
  For future usage of generative AI (genAI) systems, laypeople in Nigeria, Japan,\
  \ and Germany envisioned the future value of these systems in educational, public\
  \ service, and artistic contexts to make valuable goods (knowledge, public service,\
  \ and art creation) more accessible to everyone, thus making them public goods.\
  \ People expressed specific requirements that must be met in order for the envisioned\
  \ value to be realized. These requirements include, among others, mindful regulation,\
  \ technical robustness, and a knowledgeable population with broad access to the\
  \ technology. \n\nFor future usage of facial processing technology, laypeople in\
  \ Bolivia, India, Nigeria, Japan, and Germany envisioned these systems' value in\
  \ public safety and policing, corporate contexts, and social interactions to reduce\
  \ crime, increase security, improve efficiency, and enhance convenience in respective\
  \ processes. Similar to the results emerging from the discourses on genAI, people\
  \ considered the envisioned values to be achievable only if certain requirements\
  \ are met and particular measures implemented. These requirements include, among\
  \ others, having robust, privacy-preserving systems that are built and deployed\
  \ in accordance with regulatory frameworks. Participants also emphasized the relevance\
  \ of involving stakeholders in several governance steps and establishing awareness\
  \ of how the technology functions. The researchers observed that positive corporate\
  \ and governmental narratives influenced people's positive perspectives. Despite\
  \ expressing concerns about validity or inaccuracy, participants attributed abilities\
  \ related to the above-described envisioned values to facial processing technology.\
  \ The participants' suggestions for regulatory measures potentially mitigated their\
  \ concerns.\n\nOverall, the majority of participants demanded that AI systems be\
  \ regulated. They also highlighted the importance of keeping societies, especially\
  \ lay citizens, in the loop to discuss desirable AI development and deployment.\
  \ This was deemed especially important when discussing the appropriate mechanisms\
  \ for governing these technologies, while paying attention to local factors, such\
  \ as prevailing narratives that influence perceptions, to help question technology\
  \ narratives and technology deployment. \n"

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "With the aim of providing a space for laypeople across different\
  \ countries to explore and express their opinions on desirable AI development and\
  \ deployment, we decided on a mix of participant selection methods. The participant\
  \ outreach process employed targeted recruitment through various digital and analog\
  \ communication channels and engagement with communities (e.g., neighbourhood associations,\
  \ \u2026) that shared information about the dialogues. This approach aimed to enhance\
  \ diversity in gender, age, and AI knowledge, resulting in a self-selected pre-registered\
  \ set of the interested general population. We chose self-selection based on the\
  \ principle of universality, which reflects a participatory ideal of citizen engagement.\
  \ We implemented purposive sampling with a random component on all pre-registrations.\
  \ We ensured that each dialogue workshop group was gender-diverse and diverse across\
  \ age clusters (20-year intervals: \u2018low\u2019, \u2018middle\u2019, \u2018high\u2019\
  ) and AI knowledge levels (\u2018low\u2019, \u2018middle\u2019, \u2018high\u2019\
  ). We refer to the group of laypeople selected through this approach as a \u201C\
  mini-public,\u201D in which people gather to discuss matters of public interest."

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "The Global AI Dialogues is one of\
  \ the first efforts to engage citizens from different countries in conversations\
  \ about the future deployment of AI in specific contexts, such as generative AI\
  \ for public services. To date, 284 participants from six countries \u2013 Bolivia,\
  \ Mexico, Nigeria, India, Germany, and Japan \u2013 have participated in one-day\
  \ dialogues on generative AI and facial processing technologies. In terms of methodology,\
  \ the Global AI Dialogues feature a participatory futuring workshop paired with\
  \ individual surveys conducted at specific points in time: before, during, and after\
  \ the dialogues. During the dialogues, we use design futuring approaches to facilitate\
  \ conversations about AI. We prompt participants to consider future AI deployment,\
  \ which sets the stage for collective exploration. Participants imagine and evaluate\
  \ potential consequences based on their experiences and viewpoints. They also explore\
  \ the roles and interests of the involved stakeholders. The guided process results\
  \ in a set of measures for different actors to pursue in order to support the desirable\
  \ deployment or non-deployment of AI in the discussed application context. As a\
  \ multi-country research project, the Global AI Dialogues enable comparative analysis\
  \ of the results. This analysis was performed by an international research team,\
  \ composed of the researchers who organized and facilitated the local dialogues,\
  \ through joint analysis workshops."
