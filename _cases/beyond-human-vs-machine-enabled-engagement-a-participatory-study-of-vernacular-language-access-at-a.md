# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: This project is a participatory action
  research study examining how Rajasthani-speaking visitors engage with artefact descriptions
  at a cultural heritage museum in Jodhpur, Rajasthan. The study compared three types
  of translation of museum texts -- human translation by native speakers, machine
  translation using state-of-the-art systems, and human post-editing of machine output
  -- and evaluated visitor engagement through a mixed-methods approach combining Likert-scale
  preference ratings and comprehension MCQs. Community translators were positioned
  as co-creators rather than passive data sources. The project adhered to the CARE
  Principles for Indigenous Data Governance throughout.

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "This project is significant on several\
  \ counts.\nFirst, it engages a community that is almost entirely absent from AI\
  \ governance and NLP research: Rajasthani speakers, whose language has no official\
  \ recognition in Rajasthan, is absent from school curricula, courts, and the state\
  \ legislature, and is unrepresented in commercial machine translation systems. Second,\
  \ the project deliberately shifts evaluation authority away from technical experts\
  \ and heritage professionals toward community members themselves, i.e., using the\
  \ preferences of indegenous people as the primary measure of translation quality\
  \ rather than automated benchmark scores. Third, we also discover traces of language\
  \ colonialism which limits the capacity of native speakers to read in their own\
  \ language. and also the finding that commercial MT systems perform poorly in culturally\
  \ and heritage-specific domains, and that community judgements diverge significantly\
  \ from what automated models would predict, is directly relevant to policymakers\
  \ and developers building or procuring AI language tools for diverse communities.\
  \ Finally, the study's design, i.e, reaching communities outside formal institutions,\
  \ in the villages where they live, reflects a deliberate methodological choice to\
  \ go to where marginalised speakers are, rather than expecting them to come to the\
  \ institution. \n\nOverall, this study provides a strong case how participatory\
  \ input from indegenous communities can be irreplaceable for present AI systems."
