# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "Last year the Europeana Foundation and\
  \ the Netherlands Institute for Sound & Vision, within and on behalf of the data\
  \ space consortium, launched an Alignment Assembly on \u2018Culture for AI\u2019\
  - a participatory collective intelligence activity and consultation process with\
  \ the aim of clarifying the cultural heritage community\u2019s position on AI development,\
  \ implementation and use. During this process, we:\n-identified consensus and friction\
  \ points related to AI in the community of the common European data space for cultural\
  \ heritage;\n-identified themes 7 themes the sector need and want to position itself\
  \ on;\n-Facilitated community dialogue around the emerging dilemmas and illustrate\
  \ how the heritage sector is navigating them;\n\n\nIn order to map and examine existing\
  \ perspectives and practices in the digital heritage community, we chose to base\
  \ the process on the Alignment Assembly model developed by the Collective Intelligence\
  \ Project and inspired by the Alignment Assembly on \u201CAI and the Commons\u201D\
  \ conducted by Open Future, Creative Commons and Fundaci\xF3n Karisma in February-March\
  \ 2024. This approach combines large-scale surveys with structured conversations\
  \ to inform policy debates and guide technology development in line with shared\
  \ values.\nThe Alignment Assembly model aims to \u201Cbring technology into alignment\
  \ with collective values\u201D. The survey took place on pol.is, an online open\
  \ source survey platform described as: \u201Ca real-time system for gathering, analyzing\
  \ and understanding what large groups of people think in their own words, enabled\
  \ by advanced statistics and machine learning.\u201D The platform helps to map out\
  \ opinion groups and show the areas of consensus, friction and uncertainty.\nMoreover,\
  \ it also encourages participants to submit their own statements.\n\nThe starting\
  \ point for this participatory, collective intelligence exercise was its online\
  \ launch event in May 2025. Data space consortium partners, experts from the Europeana\
  \ Advisory Board and Steering Board were invited, as well as members of the Europeana\
  \ Network Association, Europeana Aggregators forum and experts from Netherlands\
  \ Institute for Sound & Vision. During this first participatory session, an initial\
  \ set of statements was developed,\nrefined and reviewed. A first selection of 20\
  \ statements were published on the pol.is platform and the survey was publicly launched\
  \ and widely distributed afterwards, including through Europeana website, relevant\
  \ newsletters and participants could anonymously cast their votes on published statements,\
  \ and submit their own additional statements for the community to vote on until\
  \ July 4th. After collecting and analyzing the data a paper was written after which\
  \ a workshop was held inviting participants from the different Europeana communities\
  \ to join in on an in-person workshop in order to dive deeper into some of the key\
  \ themes set out in the paper. \n"

# Headline findings
headline-findings: "Statements by participants and voted on by participants:\n\n\u201C\
  The cultural heritage sector should develop its own narrative about AI, ones that\
  \ are grounded in our values rather than focused on efficiency and commercial gains.\u201D\
  \ (91% agree, 3% disagree, 5% pass)\n\n\u201CEnvironmental responsibility standards\
  \ should be developed by the cultural heritage sector as a\nrequirement for the\
  \ use of AI tools in the data space.\u201D\n(75% agree, 12% disagree, 12% pass)\n\
  \n\u201CHeritage organisations should reject partnerships with AI companies that\
  \ fail to comply with ethical labour and data practices globally.\u201D\n(87% agree,\
  \ 6% disagree, 6% pass)\n"

why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: 'We used a relatively accessible
  replicable method to map opinions on AI on a larger scale using the pol.is platform:
  https://pol.is/report/r5mxwaf2dwthaddkkwtar. Combining this with community workshops,
  we were able to engage a large group while also going into more depth on some apparent
  themes. It gave us a good indication on where the cultural heritage community stands
  regarding AI issues in the sector and pinpointed some dilemma''s clearly. We think
  this case can serve as a replicable method for other communities interested in community
  consultation and mapping opinion groups. Because participants didn''t only get to
  vote on statement, but also submit their own statements, it made it easy for participants
  to get involved and gave us a good idea of the kinds of topics regarding AI are
  of importance. '
