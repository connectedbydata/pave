# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: 'Imaginative Intelligences was a series
  of participatory assemblies with creators from across Hollywood''s production pipeline:
  executives, gig workers, creative technologists, and scholars. Over five assemblies
  in Los Angeles, participants examined a central prompt: "creative purpose at the
  dawn of AI." Depending on the group''s makeup, each assembly coalesced around unique
  themes specific to them. For example, gig workers in session 1 chose pain as their
  theme, relating to job loss, economic precarity, and what they described as the
  distinguishing factor between humans and a model. Central to many conversations
  was the idea of creative purpose: how do creators maintain it as the value and logic
  behind it increasingly diminishes? Each session generated extensive qualitative
  material, over 30 hours of recorded conversation, later synthesized into a 35-page
  report outlining cross-industry pain points and eight design principles to inform
  more equitable creative-tech development. Imaginative Intelligences is a form of
  collective sense-making: a process that maps where narratives and beliefs around
  AI land across different sectors of the creative industry.'

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: 'This project is uniquely positioned
  at the intersection of labor and industry: it brought together Hollywood union workers,
  media executives, and creative technologists in the same participatory process,
  something no comparable method has done. The eight design principles it produced
  have already been cited in a California Senate backgrounder, demonstrating real
  policy impact. Most importantly, the project is preemptive rather than reactive.
  Where most participatory AI efforts serve as feedback mechanisms on systems already
  built, Imaginative Intelligences surfaces community-grounded principles before technology
  is optimized, created, deployed, or regulated, making it a first-mover in shaping
  more equitable creative-tech development.'
