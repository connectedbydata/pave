# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "Who gets to define what constitutes harm\
  \ in online spaces? Is it governments, platforms, or survivors on the receiving\
  \ end of that harm?  VoiceValor is a web-based auditing tool designed to uncover\
  \ blind spots in social media content moderation. It uses community-led reporting\
  \ and cultural-context analysis to identify forms of online toxicity, specifically\
  \ misogyny and hate speech, that automated systems typically miss. Marginalized\
  \ communities use VoiceValor to document and flag these algorithmic failures, while\
  \ researchers and advocates can use the platform\u2019s insights to challenge bias\
  \ and demand more equitable digital safety standards. \n\nVoiceValor enables users\
  \ to audit and correct how AI toxicity classifiers interpret local expressions of\
  \ misogyny. Using widely deployed commercial classifiers, the platform analyses\
  \ survivors\u2019 digital experiences across common harm indicators used in trust\
  \ and safety policies. It then presents transparent scores that show where algorithms\
  \ fail. \n\nUsers can flag missed harms, down-rank false positives, and provide\
  \ rich contextual insights grounded in real life experience. Outputs are explainable\
  \ at the individual comment level, allowing clear documentation for policymakers\
  \ and digital platforms. The design and implementation operationalize design justice\
  \ by introducing a new method through which voices of survivors of technology-facilitated\
  \ gender-based violence can shape the present and future of algorithmic decision-making. "
# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: "Key challenges included user interface\
  \ design, survivor safety and fatigue associated with revisiting harmful content,\
  \ uneven connectivity, and the difficulty of standardizing context across dialects\
  \ and evolving slang. Platform resistance and limited transparency from large technology\
  \ companies slowed the integration of community corrections into production moderation\
  \ systems. We are working to improve the platform's UX to increase survivors\u2019\
  \ repeat contributions and to build the VoiceValor Community to lead data governance. "

  \ but cannot understand the language of the people it has been made to regulate.\

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "Do the people closest to online harm have the most knowledge to\
  \ shape how those systems are created and designed? We believe so. VoiceValor invited\
  \ survivors and marginalized users to become the trust and safety professional auditors\
  \ from the start. Through 30 feminist, LGBTQI+, journalist, and civil society networks\
  \ across South Asia borne from our personal and professional networks, we engaged\
  \ people who had directly experienced technology-facilitated gender-based violence,\
  \ especially abuse hidden in local slang, caste and religious markers, political\
  \ codes, and identity-based insinuations. \n\nThe platform was built around their\
  \ ways of knowing. Participants could see how toxicity classifiers scored comments,\
  \ correct failures in plain language, and validate harms across 14 Global South\
  \ languages. We used low-barrier annotation workflows, explicit consent, no direct\
  \ personal identifiers, opt-out options, and trauma-sensitive engagement. To date,\
  \ survivors have contributed more than 500,000 comments and contributed over 10,000\
  \ survivor inputs documenting misclassifications and missed harms, including more\
  \ than 5,000 distinct cases of algorithmic failures linked to cultural context.\
  \ The dataset has then been used to retrain the toxicity classifiers. A community\
  \ of survivors tests every feature and flags what doesn\u2019t work. Their feedback\
  \ has led to major revisions and stronger indicators.   "

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: "As a case study serving underrepresented\
  \ communities in South Asia, VoiceValor\u2019s methodology combines digital anthropology,\
  \ participatory AI, trust and safety evaluation, data governance, and public-interest\
  \ technology design. We call the core approach thick-big data (building on Rettberg's\
  \ concept of algorithmic failure as a humanities methodology, 2022): a way of joining\
  \ the scale of digital evidence with the interpretive depth of community knowledge.\
  \ Algorithmic failure is data about where systems misunderstand the communities\
  \ they claim to protect. VoiceValor presents a novel method for co-designing and\
  \ refining AI systems by centering on the \"thick data\" of lived experience, specifically\
  \ addressing algorithmic failures of classifiers based on big data.  \n\nThrough\
  \ VoiceValor, survivors get to have say in how to shape the technology that facilitates\
  \ harm by shifting them from passive reporters to active auditors of moderation\
  \ logic who will correct algorithmic failures that silence survivors like them and\
  \ enable online abuse. These corrections are aggregated, anonymised, and fed back\
  \ to platforms to improve detection of culturally specific misogyny across 14 Global\
  \ South languages. \n\nBeyond algorithmic correction, VoiceValor also provides users\
  \ with access to support resources and 20 domain-specific AI personas. These personas\
  \ act as coaches, helping creators develop counter-narratives and strategies to\
  \ navigate digital toxicity.  \n\nOnline content moderation is one of the most consequential\
  \ governance systems of our time. It decides whose speech is protected, whose harm\
  \ is visible, and who can safely participate in digital public life. Yet it has\
  \ been built almost entirely without the participation of the communities it affects\
  \ most. VoiceValor is a democratic intervention in this system. It changes who holds\
  \ the power to define harm. Survivors\u2019 cultural expertise, linguistic fluency,\
  \ and lived understanding of how violence travels through language become the inputs\
  \ that shape AI behavior. It expands civic participation for women and gender minorities\
  \ who are currently driven out of digital spaces by unaddressed harassment. It provides\
  \ a more accurate, more representative information environment where harm is visible. "
