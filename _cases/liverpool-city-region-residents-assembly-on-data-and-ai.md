# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "In March 2025, we brought together 59\
  \ Liverpool City Region residents to debate the direction of data and AI innovation\
  \ in the region.\n\nWe supported residents to build a set of principles on what\
  \ trustworthy and beneficial data innovation looks like, that can be used to help\
  \ inform future local projects that use data with an impact on people\u2019s lives.\n\
  \nResidents took part in four in-person days split across learning and deliberation\
  \ held in Liverpool City Centre. This included presentations from experts and stakeholders\
  \ as well as interactive learning activities. An independent evaluator reviewed\
  \ all materials and sessions. On the final day residents suggested potential principles\
  \ for the Charter and ranked their preferred options. After additional resident\
  \ and stakeholder review, a final charter of 11 principles was developed.\n\nThese\
  \ principles form a Liverpool City Region Community Charter on Data and AI, one\
  \ of the first of its kind in the world. This gives innovators and researchers in\
  \ the region an actionable social license for data and AI innovation."
# What limitations to the project should be noted?
what-limitations-to-the-project-should-be-noted: 'Assemblies by their nature are exclusionary,
  only a relatively small number of people can take part and must take significant
  time out of their daily lives to do so. We designed the process to be as easy and
  welcoming as possible including a creche service, translation access, and more.
  However, we were not taken up on many of the things we offered. This suggests that
  these efforts were not enough to overcome the barriers to taking part. '

# Headline findings
headline-findings: "Residents expressed a strong hope that human dignity and integrity\
  \ would be the core value for data and AI projects. They want Liverpool to lead\
  \ the way in community-focussed innovation.\n\nThe 11 Charter principles include\
  \ ideas about beneficial data use, communication on AI, privacy and security, and\
  \ external oversight.\n\nAssembly participants are worried about a future where\
  \ the benefits of data and AI aren\u2019t felt equitably. Trustworthy and beneficial\
  \ data and AI means no community in the LCR is left behind. The principles lay the\
  \ groundwork for what residents expect of civic and health innovation in the region.\n\
  \n\nMy hope is that if used with the best interests with the public in mind, the\
  \ use of data and AI can drastically improve healthcare, public services, education,\
  \ and local services like that.\n\n\u2013 Assembly Participant, Table 6, Day 4\n\
  \nI'm just hopeful that we can be a voice for the people in the region like because\
  \ it's such a diverse group. You\u2019ve got everyone \u2013 people from all walks\
  \ of life.\n\n\u2013 Assembly Participant, Table 2 Day 3\n\n\nMy fear is the downside\
  \ \u2013 AI will just be used really to make money rather than benefiting the people\
  \ or the majority.\n\n\u2013 Assembly Participant, Table 2, Day 3\n"

# Inclusion efforts
inclusion-efforts: "We approached inclusion in three phases: building participation\
  \ before the Assembly, representation at the Assembly itself, and additional follow-up\
  \ with unrepresented groups. \n\nBefore\nIn the lead up to the Assembly we ran five\
  \ community data conversations with equity seeking groups specifically looking to\
  \ represent under-engaged voices that might not be represented in the Assembly.\
  \ This included residents from immigrant communities, very low income/unstably housed\
  \ residents, residents with significant caring responsibilities and/or disability\
  \ status, elderly residents, and community volunteers. Quotes and themes from these\
  \ residents were used in the Assembly activities to ensure their voices were represented\
  \ in the Charter. \n\nDuring\n59 residents took part in all four days of the Assembly.\
  \ They were chosen to represent both the six local authorities and the diversity\
  \ of the LCR. We chose to use the term \u2018resident\u2019 instead of \u2018citizen\u2019\
  . The term \u2018citizen\u2019 excludes residents who may not have full UK citizenship.\
  \ \u2018Resident\u2019 defines who is eligible and includes everyone who lives in\
  \ the region. \n\nResidents were invited by post to register to take part and then\
  \ selected through a demographic lottery. We purposively selected residents by local\
  \ area, AI literacy, ethnicity, age, gender, disability, and education level. We\
  \ sent out a second wave of recruitment materials to residents in the lowest SES\
  \ post codes. Invitation materials were in English, Polish, and Arabic. \n\nAfter\n\
  We also followed the Assembly up with a Youth AI Summit and engagement with children\
  \ on the Charter to get additional feedback. "

# Why is this project important to include in the case book?
why-is-this-project-important-to-include-in-the: 'Building on a rich history of participatory
  practice and citizens'' assemblies across the UK - this entry shows an important
  example of taking the results of an assembly and directly applying them in government
  and NHS contexts. The final charter is written by the residents and adopted in full
  by local government, local NHS, and the university of Liverpool.


  In addition this participatory process was built on layers of previous participatory
  work that fed directly in to the Assembly, that includes:

  -Community conversations on data/AI with equity seeking groups in the LCR

  -Peer research on developing data hubs in community in the LCR

  -Focus groups on health data and data governance with residents sampled across equity-seeking
  criteria

  -Podcast development with residents on current AI uses'
