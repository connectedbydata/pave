# Provide a brief description of the project
provide-a-brief-description-of-the-project: "As artificial intelligence (AI) increasingly\
  \ shapes everyday life, there is growing awareness that citizen participation in\
  \ AI development and governance is essential. This is particularly critical for\
  \ the most vulnerable communities \u2013 those least likely to be involved in participatory\
  \ practices yet most likely to be impacted by AI systems (Oman & Cannizzaro, 2025).\n\
  \nThis paper presents findings from participatory action research (PAR) workshops\
  \ on AI inclusion, conducted with members of financially disadvantaged communities\
  \ in the United Kingdom (UK). The central research question of the project is: how\
  \ can we understand AI inclusion through dialogue with those experiencing financial\
  \ disadvantage?\n\nOver two series of workshops (4 workshops each), we engaged participants\
  \ of mixed ages (including older adults) in Inverness (Scotland) and predominantly\
  \ younger participants in London (England). Partnering with local charities, we\
  \ co-created spaces to explore what AI means to these communities and identify the\
  \ challenges and opportunities they perceive. Our methodological approach drew inspiration\
  \ from 'Pedagogy of the Oppressed' (Freire, 1972), 'Teaching Community: A Pedagogy\
  \ of Hope' (hooks, 2003), and Fals Borda\u2019s (1987) ideas on participatory action\
  \ research \u2013 demonstrating that methodologies originating in the Global South\
  \ can be meaningfully applied in the Global North, learning from the experiences\
  \ of the \u2018South in the North\u2019 (Medrado & Verdegem, 2024).\n\nOur findings\
  \ reveal that after sharing and discussing everyday life AI experiences (or the\
  \ lack thereof), the participants developed nuanced understandings of the risks\
  \ and benefits of AI. A critical aspect of participatory AI research with members\
  \ of vulnerable communities is the barrier of inaccessible language around these\
  \ technologies. Many participants faced literacy challenges, compelling us to reconsider\
  \ how we discuss AI within these communities. This insight highlights the effectiveness\
  \ of community teaching and learning, even when participants have limited knowledge\
  \ and experience with AI.\n\nThe next phase of the project involves translating\
  \ these co-created insights on AI inclusion into actionable proposals that can promote\
  \ AI literacy, enabling participants to make informed decisions about whether and\
  \ how to engage with AI, as well as the support they need. This research emphasises\
  \ that genuine AI inclusion requires not only participatory research but also concrete\
  \ pathways from dialogue to action \u2013 ensuring that the voices of financially-disadvantaged\
  \ communities shape AI futures rather than simply being documented.\n\nThis work\
  \ contributes to participatory AI research by centering marginalised voices, adapting\
  \ Global South methodologies for Northern contexts, and identifying practical barriers\
  \ to inclusion that must be addressed for equitable AI development."
