# **Focus**

In creating this case book, collating examples of AI-focussed public **voice** and **participatory engagement** activities, we have a number of goals:

1. **To inform inclusive practice** by inviting cases to describe the ‘Who’, ‘What’ and ‘How’ of engagement, and to outline how choices about recruitment, methods or project design have supported greater diversity or involvement of marginalised groups in meaningful engagement on AI. 

   *Featured cases* offer examples of participatory practice in new contexts, with well considered methods, or particular efforts to give power to marginalised voices. 

2. **To amplify voices** from communities across the world who have been seeking to understand, express opinions on, and take action to shape, AI. 

   For many case book entries we have extracted key recommendations and messages from public participants, and we’ve created a playlist of project videos where participants share their views. We’re experimenting with different ways to display these \- inviting power-holders to better engage with public views on AI. 

3. **To inspire new participation projects** by demonstrating how different projects have found best-fit methods for supporting participatory approaches to AI, showing participatory AI projects taking place across the globe, and highlighting gaps that need to be filled. 

   Many case book entries include links to supporting method guides, toolkits and learning resources created to help participants have informed and empowered conversations about AI. 

The case book is a living project. The first release was prepared for the 2026 UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance. We are exploring ways to continue to maintain the case book in the year ahead. 

# Case Book Methodology

The case book contains three kinds of entry:

| Featured cases | Full cases | Mapping entries |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| These full cases demonstrate participatory practice in new contexts, with well considered methods, and/or with particular efforts to give power to marginalised voices.  | Full cases include details of the ‘Who’, ‘What’ and ‘How’ of a participatory process, along with an edited case description and links to further resources.  | These provide basic information about a participatory AI project that has taken place. They may be developed into full cases in future.  |

The case book has been created through crowdsourcing and desk-research.

* We invite people involved in, or aware of, particular participatory AI projects to submit these for considerations, and we identify further potential projects through desk research.  
* We triage each submission to decide whether to create a full case or mapping entry.  
* We review submissions, carry out desk research and invite additional input from contributors in order to populate the full case study template with structured data and edited narrative. 

In order to recognise the diversity of participatory practice around the world, we adopt a broad definition of what constitutes participatory practice, guided by a focus on participation as activity that has the potential to ultimately lead to power redistribution and/or technical, social and political change. This includes focussing on:

* **Democratic participation**, distinct from formal political participation (e.g. voting), but incorporating activities that bring people together to learn about, discuss, and articulate views about, shared topics.   
* **Scientific participation**, including a wide range of forms of participatory research that give power to marginalised voices. 

The case book does not seek to be a comprehensive dataset of all possible participatory processes, and so our curation decisions are also guided by showcasing the **geographic**, **methodological** and **thematic** diversity of participatory AI, and paying particular attention to processes that demonstrate attention on **inclusive practice**.

## AI Use Statement

We have used Large Language Model AI tools in the following ways as part of this project:

### Research and presentation support

* Members of the research team may use LLMs as one element of information search and retrieval (for example, to identify where certain case study fields may be answered within project documents). All evidence is cross-checked against the original source before being entered into the Case Book.  
* When case studies are submitted by contributors, we ask whether AI tools were used to generate summary text, and whether these summaries have been checked for accuracy by a human. We cross-check against project documents to ensure details are accurate.  
* We created a small web service to generate consistent case summaries, and to help check that key data fields are populated accurately. Two options for generated summaries are presented to editors in the case editing interface, and may be copied and edited into the case description.

Only information already in the public domain is fed into LLMs for this.

### Code generation

The creation of this website, and some of the python scripts used to manage the project, was assisted using Google Antigravity (coding agent).
